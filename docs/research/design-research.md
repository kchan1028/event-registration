# Make registration decisions clear before race day

Research date: **November 16, 2026**. Status: **Proposed design research for review**, not approved requirements, implementation work, a replacement roadmap, or evidence of live-event readiness. Project baseline: `5f5ee673ebaca9e2cfdb2c5b9c91b02c1caa2f5a`.

## Executive recommendation

Prioritize comprehension and recovery across four decisions: **which category fits, what the complete purchase includes, what remains before racing, and what a volunteer may safely do next**. The local build already contains structured eligibility, package pricing, a visible package comparison, an opt-in matching-race shortcut, and server-side race-readiness safeguards. The next design work should test whether beginners and staff understand those distinctions, especially when license information is unknown, several entries share a start, or a paid rider remains blocked. Public patterns across Platform A, Platform B and Platform C support explicit participant ownership, itemized purchases and consequence previews, but do not establish that a proposed design will reduce errors or support workload. Prototype the high-risk decisions before expanding self-service or changing the visual system. Keep current requirements and release gates unchanged until proposals are reviewed and validated.

F01 is [one existing interview record](../discovery/interviews/D01.md), not four participants. The same person answered as promoter, registration lead, treasurer and rider. Reconciliation, bib assignment and export preparation were described as time-consuming, but estimates were not checked against logs or files. Accepted federation/waiver policy remains a separate dependency.

## Proposed racer, guardian and volunteer flows

These are **review proposals**, preserving the domain boundaries in [category concepts](../../product.md#keep-the-concepts-separate) and [race-day operations](../../product.md#5-race-day-operations). Existing safeguards remain authoritative. New screens, help routes, household batching or self-service actions are not implied to exist.

| Journey | Proposed decision sequence | Recovery and safety boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Racer | Identify event/days and discipline → understand required rider facts → view all categories or request matching choices → inspect each start/length/price → choose at most one category per shared start, with multiple compatible entries where the schedule permits → compare package coverage of one entry per component race, additional entries and total → review participant/signature → review the complete purchase → inspect confirmation and outstanding requirements | Unknown license facts lead to help/review, not invented qualification. Preserve unaffected choices on errors. State blocking conflicts versus recovery warnings. Pending/failed payment is not a confirmed entry; retry must not create duplicates. |
| Guardian | Identify the child whose entry is being prepared → review that child's age/category facts and choices → identify the payer and authorized signer separately → complete child-specific requirements → review child, races, consent status and total → receive a child-specific requirements checklist | An adult paying does not automatically authorize signing. A minor in an open/adult category still needs the applicable guardian process. Keep a second child's context separate; do not imply the present one-rider order is already a multi-rider cart. Missing consent needs a defined owner/deadline and cannot silently disappear after payment. |
| Volunteer | Select event/day and confirm current data source → locate identity with search/code → verify intended entries → read ready/blocked/review outcome → issue/confirm the existing or permitted bib only when allowed → move to next rider | Repeated scans must not issue another bib. Blocked riders go to an authorized exception desk with a reason; payment alone is insufficient. A printable snapshot remains dated fallback data, not live state or proof of offline synchronization. |

For changes, prototype a separate review step stating original entry, requested entry, availability/eligibility checks, amount owed/refunded, fees retained, entry retained/withdrawn, package/bib implications and resulting list revision. If policy disallows an action, explain the deadline or approval route. A signature must not be transferred to another person. For exports, separate preparing a file from the recipient accepting the current version.

### Content and screen hierarchy

| Surface | First decision to support | Content hierarchy proposed for review |
|---|---|---|
| Event/discovery | Is this the right event and day? | Event/discipline/date/time zone/location, followed by race choices, registration state and policy; label staging separately from actual start time |
| Category choice | Which choices fit, and what is uncertain? | Required facts with plain-language help, complete schedule as default, explicit matching control, shared-start grouping, per-category qualification/review explanation and availability |
| Entry summary | What exactly am I buying? | Named rider, race/day/category, included package components, optional extra entries, warnings, discounts/fees and grand total; missing components remain actionable |
| Guardian/details | Whose entry and whose responsibility? | Persistent child context; participant contact versus guardian/payer/signer roles; required fields and signer-specific error recovery |
| Review/payment | What will happen when I commit? | Full purchase contents and total, accepted waiver/policy version, capacity/hold consequences and an explicit next action; distinguish pending, declined and failed recovery |
| Confirmation | What succeeded, and what remains? | Payment and entry result, per-entry readiness, action/owner/deadline for outstanding requirements, then receipt and identification code |
| Change/refund | What money and entry state will change? | Before/after comparison, policy/deadline, financial and capacity effects, retained record and recovery route |
| Check-in/export | What may staff safely hand over or send? | Identity and event/day, readiness/bib action, blocker handoff, data freshness/scope/revision, then administrative detail according to role |

Use the existing [visual system](../../DESIGN.md) and [content guidance](../content-guide.md): plain terms, readable dense data, visible focus and text alongside color. Prefer explaining a decision beside its action over adding a new dashboard, animation or decorative surface. Whether these changes improve comprehension is H until tested.

## Proposed unmoderated validation plan

**No recruitment or sessions for this proposed study have occurred.** This is a proposed formative protocol for an inert prototype with synthetic data, not permission to expose the local application, create real registrations, charge money or alter an account. Use the existing [usability kit](../usability/README.md) and [task definitions](../usability/tasks.md) as scenario and governance references, not as an operational unmoderated script. Its facilitator-only local setup and verbal-consent procedure cannot serve remote participants. Run this protocol only on an owner-approved inert prototype with self-service instructions, synthetic records and no live transaction or account routes. It does not replace the existing participant gate, operational rehearsal or accessibility audit.

Preserve the already Planned [initial round](../usability/README.md#berkeley-weekend-initial-round) of 3–5 riders/parents and 2–3 organizers or volunteers. The following is an optional formative follow-up, only after the owner approves its sequence and learns from that initial round; it neither replaces it nor silently runs alongside it. For that follow-up, propose 14 unique adults: four novice/uncertain-category riders, three experienced multi-entry riders, three adults who register juniors, and four current or new check-in volunteers. Roles can overlap, but count people once and report role/task denominators separately. Assign only relevant tasks, avoid presenting the same answer first in a tutorial, and include keyboard/large-text/assistive-technology use where participants choose to disclose a preference. No minor needs to participate: child scenarios use fictional profiles. This is a purposive formative sample, not a statistically representative estimate.

Before any invitation, the owner must approve the study owner, sequence, safe prototype access, data minimization, storage and retention. No recruitment has occurred. The opening participant screen must explain the study purpose without teaching the desired answers, voluntary participation, what task data will be collected, access/retention, withdrawal and a contact route. Capture affirmative participation consent before showing tasks; declining ends the study without collecting task responses. Ask separately for optional recording consent, defaulted off. Refusing recording must not prevent participation. Capture no faces or voices by default, and no real license/contact/payment/child data. Keep identity, consent and recordings outside Git; publish deidentified results only. Resolve retention and deletion arrangements before collection.

Show a synthetic-data warning at entry and beside any editable field. Keep **End study**, **Skip task** and a self-stop/help control available throughout. Explain that stopping or skipping needs no justification and is not a personal failure. Ending stops further task collection and shows the approved withdrawal/contact route; skipped, stopped and interrupted tasks remain distinct outcomes. A participant who encounters a live route, enters real information, feels distress or cannot access a task should stop immediately using these controls. Prototype routes must be inert and checked before release to participants. These are proposed study safeguards, not claims about a hosted study already available.

This is asynchronous: nobody watches live or rescues the participant. In-prototype help is normal product use and does not disqualify success; record its use separately. Ask after each task whether outside help was used, such as another person or an external explanation. Flag those attempts as externally assisted and analyze them separately from unaided attempts. Do not treat an unanswered assistance question as proof of no help. The study owner reviews submitted results afterward and follows the approved deletion/escalation process if an unsafe or unintended submission is reported.

Use task instructions that describe the goal rather than the target control. Collect click path, correct/incorrect completion, abandoned attempts, validation/recovery errors, in-prototype help and self-reported external assistance, elapsed time, confidence and an unaided written explanation of cost/contents/readiness. Keep confidence separate from correctness. An unavailable prototype function is a study limitation, not participant failure. Time results should be medians and ranges with raw denominators, not predicted labor savings; a remote prototype does not measure a physical race queue.

Analyze raw successful, incorrect, skipped, stopped and externally assisted counts by task and cohort, with explicit denominators and recurring error patterns. Do not pool small cohorts to conceal a safety misunderstanding or infer prevalence from this purposive sample. Use a proposed zero-unsafe-outcome triage rule: any unsafe clearance belief, blocked bib issue, wrong signer acceptance, unintended package or misunderstood charge triggers owner review and a recorded design/rule question. This is a research escalation rule, not a new release gate or a pass percentage. The existing formal Phase 1 criteria remain unchanged and cannot be satisfied by this report or an optional formative follow-up. Re-test critical revisions with new participants, record prototype versions and preserve incomplete attempts in analysis.

Exclude unavailable prototype functions from participant-error counts and report them as study limitations. A self-stop for consent withdrawal, accidental real data, a live route, distress or an access barrier is never scored as rescued success. Review outcomes asynchronously; no facilitator intervention is assumed. This plan cannot establish legal waiver validity, federation/payment acceptance, screen-reader conformance, real-phone/daylight performance, concurrency, outage recovery or timer round-trip success; those require separate approved checks and rehearsals.

The most important unresolved issue is whether people distinguish a plausible race choice, a completed purchase and permission to race.
