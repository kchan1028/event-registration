# Facilitator script

Read the parts in quotation marks aloud, in your own words if that sounds more natural. Keep the same meaning, especially the consent questions and the reminder that we are testing the software, not the person.

Two people run a session: a **facilitator** who talks with the participant, and a **note-taker** who fills in the [observation sheet](observation-sheet.md) and times the tasks. One person can do both, but notes will be thinner.

## Before the session

"Thanks for helping. We're building a prototype for race registration and race-day check-in, and we want to see where it's confusing before anyone relies on it. We're testing the software, not you. If something is hard, that's exactly what we need to find."

"Everything you'll see is made up: the events, the riders, the prices. Please don't type any real personal information. If a screen asks for something, use the fake details we give you or invent them."

"Nothing you do here registers anyone or moves any money. Payment is simulated."

Ask, and record the answers on the sheet:

1. "Is it OK if I take notes while you work?"
2. "Is it OK to record the screen? It helps us review later. We won't record your face or voice unless you say yes to that too." (Optional. A no is fine.)
3. "Your name won't appear in anything we publish; we use a code like P03. You can stop at any time without giving a reason. OK to go ahead?"

Then explain think-aloud:

"As you work, please say what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and anything that surprises you. If you'd normally ask someone a question, ask it out loud. I may not answer right away, because I want to see what you would do on your own, but I'll help if you get stuck."

Ask the warm-up questions for the role (from [tasks.md](tasks.md)) before starting the first task.

## During each task

1. Read the task scenario exactly as written in [tasks.md](tasks.md). Hand the participant a printed copy so they can re-read it.
2. Start the timer when you finish reading. Stop it when the participant says they are done or reaches the success state.
3. Stay quiet and neutral. Do not nod, sigh, or say "good" when they find the right control.
4. After the task, ask the follow-up question for that task, then: "On a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 is very hard and 5 is very easy, how easy was that?"

### Prompts and rescues

The difference decides whether a task counts as unassisted.

**Neutral prompts** keep the participant talking without giving information. They do not change the outcome code (a success after prompts is SP).

- "What are you looking for?"
- "What would you do next?"
- "What do you expect that button to do?"
- "Take your time."
- Repeating the task scenario word for word.

**A rescue** gives information or takes an action that moves the task forward. Any rescue makes the outcome A.

- Pointing at, naming, or hinting at a control ("try the menu on the left").
- Explaining a term or a rule ("a start group is where categories share a field").
- Correcting a wrong choice ("that's the wrong race").
- Touching the keyboard or mouse.

Offer a rescue when the participant has been stuck for about two minutes, asks to stop, or is getting distressed. Say: "Let me help you past this part so we can keep going." Note exactly what you said.

If the participant finishes with a wrong result and believes they are done (for example, they chose the wrong race), do not correct them during the task. Score it F, then ask the follow-up question. You may show them the right path at the end of the session.

### Things to note every time

- Where they hesitated, and what they said at that moment.
- Wrong turns: anything clicked or opened that was not on the path.
- Words they did not understand or used differently ("wave" for start group, "division" for category).
- Anything they believed that was not true (for example, "so I'm all set to race" when license review is pending).

## After all tasks

Ask these, then any role-specific debrief questions from [tasks.md](tasks.md):

1. "What was the most confusing part?"
2. "Was there anything you expected to see that wasn't there?"
3. "Compared with how you register (or run registration, or check riders in) today, would this be easier, harder, or about the same? Why?"
4. "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"

Close: "Thank you. This really helps. We'll use what you found to fix the confusing parts before anyone uses this for a real race."

## After the participant leaves

1. Finish the observation sheet while it is fresh: outcome codes, times, ease scores, quotes.
2. Mark anything that might be a [critical blocker](README.md#score-the-sessions).
3. Stop the app. Keep the session's database file for review; it holds only fictional data.
