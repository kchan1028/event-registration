## Top time costs (D01's words, summarized)

1. **Reconciliation: about 8 hours per event.** The method described is "total registration fees minus processing fees". The time goes to building reports, matching refunds, and accounting for merchandise sales (follow-up).
2. **Bib assignment across multiple fields is "very time consuming": about 1 hour, depending on entries** (follow-up). Bibs are assigned in the registration system, with each field given its own block inside 1 to 1000.
3. **Start-list export: 30 to 60 minutes** to configure the CSV, or about 5 minutes when a previous template is reused.

## Race director

- Setup starts with deciding categories, field sizes, which fields to combine, and whether combined fields are scored separately.
- The road race is copied from last year. The criterium needs a redesign so riders can race more than once.
- Field limits follow the USA Cycling (USAC) rule D01 cited: 75 riders for Novice, 100 for all other fields.
- Prices come from expected entries (based on last year) and sponsor money.
- After opening, the schedule can change with entry numbers. Affected riders get a refund or move to a different time or field.
- No race-weekend problem is known to trace back to registration.
- Setup hours: about 1 hour when only prices change; a few hours when fields, categories, or times change.
- A successor would need documentation.
- What a replacement must keep: unsure.
- Would be unwilling to switch if it hurt the event's visibility in search engines and AI answers (search engine optimization (SEO), answer engine optimization (AEO), generative engine optimization (GEO)).

## Registration lead

- No recent registration problem D01 knows of.
- Most common problems: license issues and duplicate entries. No counts given. Tracked in the registration system.
- Unconfirmed license: "we can't because USAC rule". Whether this means the rider cannot race, or that the organizer cannot override, is unclear.
- Start lists are exported from the registration system (see time costs).
- Race-morning check-in is mostly day-of registration. Volunteers confirm the race license and waiver.
- Rider messages arrive weekly around an event. Common topics not given.
- What to hand off first: no answer.

## Treasurer

- Payouts arrive by ACH within a week.
- The organizer approves refunds. Riders can transfer entries themselves.
- The club pays no fees. Riders pay about 10% on top (D01's figure, 2026-11-14; not checked against a provider's published fees).
- No disputes or chargebacks.
- Reports needed: USA Cycling, taxes, and analytics for marketing.
- The club would accept a different payment provider or payout schedule. Conditions not stated.

## Rider

- Last registration was "simple and easy" on BikeReg. The total price was clear.
- Category is chosen from the USAC license.
- Has entered both days of a weekend or more than one race, and has transferred an entry.
- Check-in was quick by showing the email confirmation.
- Has never contacted an organizer about registration.
- Would change: make it easier to select every race the rider can enter, and easier to find omnium or series packages.

## Follow-up answers (2026-11-15)

1. **Reconciliation:** the 8 hours go to building reports, matching refunds, and merchandise sales.
2. **Bibs:** about 1 hour, depending on how many riders register. The bib range is 1 to 1000, and each field gets its own block (for example starting at 100, 200, 300, or 100, 350, 400), which has to be planned around expected field sizes.
3. **Registration system:** BikeReg, both as promoter and as rider.
4. **Search visibility:** the worry is that riders don't know another platform and wouldn't find or trust the event there, not the club's own site ranking.
5. **Shared fields:** several Masters fields combine categories, for example Masters 35+ Cat 3/4 and Masters 35+ Cat 1/2/3, "and many more". Whether BikeReg or a person enforced the shared limit was not answered.
6. **Discounts:** omnium package, junior pricing, collegiate pricing, and discounted women's entry. Setting these up is hard because they need discount codes to be generated.
7. **Unconfirmed license:** the rider cannot register, which D01 attributes to a USAC rule. So license problems are stopped at registration, not at check-in.
8. **Counts:** about 30 day-of registrations per event. License issue and duplicate counts not given.

## Closing answers (2026-11-15)

1. **Shared-field limits:** a person enforces them, not BikeReg. Whether BikeReg could cap a combined field with different setup is not yet checked.
2. **License issues and duplicates:** none per event. This corrects the earlier answer that named them as the most common problems; D01 meant these are the problem types that can occur, not ones that happen.
3. **Unconfirmed-license rule:** D01 did not cite a specific rule. The next step is to contact USA Cycling about API access for license checks, which is already covered by question 7 in the [USAC questions](../../usac-questions.md).

No D01 follow-ups remain open from the closing round. The USAC contact owner was assigned on 2026-11-15 (see [external requirements](../external-requirements.md#status)).

## Racer follow-ups (2026-11-15)

D01 answered eight more questions as a rider. They are still D01's view, not a new interview: they deepen the rider answers above but do not count toward the five riders or parents the roadmap asks for.

1. **Choosing races:** a single race is easy, and so is an omnium or stage race sold as a package. It is hard to enter **two races out of three** in a stage race or weekend: D01 has to scroll to find each race.
2. **Packages:** found by scrolling up and down the event page. D01 did not say whether they ever missed one.
3. **Device:** usually a phone, sometimes a computer. How many days before the race, and whether they ever registered on race morning, was not answered.
4. **Fees:** a processing or USA Cycling fee has never surprised D01 at checkout.
5. **Sign-in:** an emailed sign-in code **during checkout would put D01 off** (answered "during checkout yes"). A one-time authenticator-app setup is **not acceptable for riders**: "authenticator app will be a turn down for consumer" (answered later on 2026-11-15). This conflicts with product.md, which requires MFA for riders before the live pilot and lists an authenticator app as one option; the rider factor is an open decision for the owner.
6. **Full fields:** D01 has never joined a waitlist or missed a race because a field filled.
7. **Transfers:** D01 enters the other person's email so the entry is sent to them to register.
8. **Check-in:** an email confirmation or a QR code works. D01 would also like to **show their USAC license by QR code or email link** at check-in.

What this suggests (observations to test with other riders, not requirements):

- Partial multi-race entry (two of three races) needs races grouped by day and race so a rider can find and pick each one without scrolling. Compare with product.md's multi-race selection and the "easier to select every race" answer above.
- Asking for sign-in in the middle of checkout is the moment that costs riders. Sign-in should happen before the rider starts picking races, or be remembered on the phone from an earlier visit. This matters for the Phase 2 email sign-in work.
- Phone-first: every rider screen must work well on a phone (already required in product.md and the coding guide).
- License display at check-in is not in product.md. It depends on what USA Cycling allows (see the [USAC questions](../../usac-questions.md)).
